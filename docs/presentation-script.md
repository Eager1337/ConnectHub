# ConnectHub — Presentation Script
**Suggested duration:** 7–9 minutes  
**Replace:** group member names and any unverified project details before presenting.

## Slide 1 — Title (30 seconds)
“Good day, lecturer and everyone. We are presenting ConnectHub, our Human Computer Interaction project. ConnectHub is a proposed university community platform designed to make it easier for students to discover campus information, find communities and take part in events. Our focus is the user interface and the way students interact with it.”

## Slide 2 — The problem (60 seconds)
“Students may receive campus information through different channels such as class groups, personal messages, printed notices and word of mouth. This can make it difficult to keep track of activities or discover the right study group. Our design question is: how might one clear interface help students discover relevant campus information and participate more easily? We will validate the exact needs through student research rather than assume every student has the same experience.”

## Slide 3 — Brainstorming and selected idea (60 seconds)
“We considered three ideas. The first was ConnectHub, a university community platform. The second was StudyMate, which matches students for peer study. The third was QueueLess, which helps students view and join campus service queues. We selected ConnectHub because it combines several related campus tasks and can be prototyped and evaluated within the course timeframe. Its core interface flows can be demonstrated without building a full production social network.”

## Slide 4 — Goals and objectives (45 seconds)
“Our goal is to design and evaluate a clear, accessible and responsive campus interface. Our objectives are to understand student needs, organise the main campus tasks, build a working front-end prototype, apply HCI principles and evaluate representative tasks with users.”

## Slide 5 — Research methodology (75 seconds)
“Our proposed methodology uses short semi-structured interviews and task-based usability evaluation. We plan to recruit approximately five to eight students through convenience sampling, with voluntary participation. Interviews will explore how students currently find announcements, events and study partners. During usability evaluation, participants will be asked to find an event, save a post, publish an update, discover a group, RSVP and mark notifications as read. We will summarise interview notes by theme and record task completion, errors and participant comments. We will only report findings after the sessions have actually taken place.”

## Slide 6 — Prototype overview (45 seconds)
“The prototype is built with HTML, CSS and JavaScript. It includes six main areas: Home, Discover, Campus Events, Messages, Saved Posts and Notifications. The design uses sample content and local browser interactions. It is a demonstration of the interface, not a live platform with real student accounts or messaging.”

## Slide 7 — Demonstration (90 seconds)
“First, on the Home page, we can view campus posts and upcoming events. We can type a new update in the composer and publish it. Next, we can like or save a post. The save action changes its label, and the post appears under Saved Posts. In Campus Events, we can review the event details and select RSVP. We can also open Notifications and mark the sample updates as read. These state changes provide feedback so the user can understand the result of each action.”

## Slide 8 — HCI principles (60 seconds)
“We applied visibility of system status by highlighting the current page and changing action states. We used consistency through repeated card and button patterns. Recognition rather than recall is supported by keeping section names visible. User control is supported by allowing actions such as likes and saved status to be reversed. We also used responsive layouts so the interface adapts to smaller screens. These are design intentions that still need to be checked through user evaluation.”

## Slide 9 — Evaluation and limitations (45 seconds)
“Our evaluation plan asks students to complete representative tasks while we observe completion, hesitation and errors. The prototype uses sample data and does not include authentication, a real database, moderation or live messaging. We will document these limitations and use genuine participant feedback to improve the design.”

## Slide 10 — Conclusion (30 seconds)
“To conclude, ConnectHub is a practical HCI prototype that brings campus information and community activities into one interface. The project demonstrates how user-centred research, interface design and evaluation can be combined. Thank you for listening. We are ready for your questions.”

## Likely questions and suggested answers
**Why did you choose ConnectHub?**  
“It addresses several connected campus tasks and has interface flows that can be implemented and evaluated within the course timeframe.”

**Is ConnectHub a complete working social network?**  
“No. It is a front-end HCI prototype using sample data. Real authentication, storage, messaging infrastructure and moderation are outside this assignment prototype's scope.”

**How will you know whether the design is usable?**  
“We will ask representative students to complete defined tasks, observe completion and errors, and use their comments to identify improvements.”

**What security measures would a real version need?**  
“A production version would need institutional authentication, role-based access, secure transport and storage, privacy controls, reporting and moderation processes, data minimisation and a clear retention policy. These are future system requirements, not implemented features in this prototype.”

**What would you improve next?**  
“After user testing, we would prioritise the most frequent usability problems, improve accessibility, validate the information architecture and only then plan backend integration.”
